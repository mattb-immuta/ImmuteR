immuta_accounts.html is a single, self-contained web page that brings together everything Customer Success tracks about each Immuta customer: contract and renewal data from Salesforce, product usage from Looker, support history, open escalations from Jira, and the SaaS migration pipeline. It answers three questions:

  1. Who are our customers and what’s moving? (Accounts and Engagement)
  2. What are they actually using? (Adoption)
  3. How is it running? (Performance)

This guide explains how to read the page. You don’t need any special tools, just open the file in a browser.

Getting the page and knowing how fresh it is

  • Updated date (top right). When the underlying data was pulled, not when the file was opened or saved. If it looks old, the numbers are old.
  • User Guide and Definitions. Opens this guide in a new tab. The guide is built into the dashboard file, so it works offline too.
  • Download Update. Downloads the latest published copy from Google Drive. Use this to get current data.
  • Refresh. Re-runs the data pipeline. This only works on the owner’s machine (it needs a local R process running), so other readers should use Download Update instead. If you click Refresh without that process running, a pop-up explains what’s needed. You can close it.
  • Compare to. Shows which earlier snapshot the Changes tab is compared against. The newest snapshot is selected by default. Picking a different date only takes effect when the owner refreshes the page.

The page works offline once downloaded. All data is embedded in the file.

Controls that work everywhere

Control What it does
Search box Filters by account name on every tab. On Escalations it also matches the issue summary and Jira key.
Region / CSM pills Narrow the view to one region or one Customer Success Manager. These selections are shared: pick “EMEA” on Accounts and it stays selected on Renewals, Adoption, CS Coverage, and so on.
Clickable account names In any table, clicking an account name takes you to that account’s detail card on the Accounts tab and opens it.
Sortable columns Click a column header to sort, and click it again to reverse.
Count badges The numbers on each tab show how many rows are in view after your filters are applied.

Conventions

  • — (em-dash) means the value is missing or not set in the source system. It doesn’t mean zero.
  • Stoplight colors are used for every rating on the page: Green (healthy), Amber (moderate), Red (high risk), Black (critical churn risk).
  • Hover over a rating badge to see its full definition.
  • Fiscal quarters. Immuta’s fiscal year starts in February, and quarters are named for the calendar year the fiscal year ends in. For example, March 2026 falls in Q1-2027, and Nov 2026 to Jan 2027 is Q4-2027.
  • Onboarding means lifecycle stage S1: Installation and Config. Low usage is expected there, so onboarding accounts are marked wherever it matters: a square marker on the scatter plots, and an amber highlight on CS Coverage and the Adoption table.

1. Accounts and Engagement

Five sub-tabs: Accounts, Renewals, Expansions, Changes, and CS Coverage.

Accounts

One card for every customer account. You can sort them Name (A–Z) (with an A–Z jump bar) or End Date (grouped by the fiscal quarter of the account’s next renewal, with the ARR renewing in each quarter).

Card header (collapsed view):

  • Four-square glyph. A quick read of adoption: PBAC, Request, Comply, and Agentic, each colored by its stoplight rating. Hover a square to see its label. The PBAC square is the PBAC-only rating (see PBAC rating vs. product adoption rating).
  • Account name. An (M) after the name means the account runs Immuta on more than one platform in Production.
  • ARR. Contract ARR rollup.
  • Links. Current Consumption (Looker) and Customer Central.
  • Tags. Region, deployment (SaaS, SM: Supported, or SM: Unsupported, where SM = self-managed), and the churn quarter(s) of open renewals.

Click a card to expand it. It has four tabs.

Overview has five columns:

Column Contents
Account basics Contract ARR, Region, CSM, Account Owner, Field CTO
Adoption: evidence Lifecycle Stage, PBAC rating with its evidence (platforms in use, PBAC features in use, complexity score out of 7), Registered vs. Licensed users, and the consumption ratio (registered ÷ licensed). The ratio is colored red below 25%, amber from 25% to 50%, and green at 50% or above.
Adoption: ratings A small PBAC plot showing where this account sits on complexity vs. consumption, plus the Request, Agentic, and Comply ratings
Performance Deployment type, Immuta version, Connections migration progress, support incidents in the last 90 and 360 days, CustX escalations in the last 90 days, open escalations (highlighted if any), and a tile for each open escalation linking to Jira
Engagement Executive Buyer and Champion, each with relationship strength and influence (Strong, Neutral, or Weak), last and next QBR dates, and the latest QBR note

On the Platforms line, platforms shown in grey exist only in Dev or Test, not Production.

Architecture shows SaaS or self-hosted, an Unsupported Version warning where it applies, and the active products and quantities on order.

Renewals lists open renewal opportunities with amount, contract end date, churn quarter, and next steps. Opportunity names link to Salesforce.

Expansions lists open expansion opportunities with owner, amount, fiscal period, and next steps.

Renewals

A single table of every open renewal across all accounts. Summary figures at the top show the number of open renewals, total ARR, and the number of accounts. By default it’s sorted by End Date and grouped under churn-quarter headings with subtotals. Sorting by any other column gives a flat list. The Risk column is colored High, Moderate, or Low.

Expansions

The same layout for open expansion opportunities, with summary figures for the number of expansions, pipeline value, and number of accounts. When sorted by Close Date, rows are grouped by fiscal quarter.

Changes

Shows what’s different since the snapshot named in Compare to. The header gives the comparison date and a count for each area. Each section can be collapsed and uses badges: + added, − removed, ~ changed.

Section What it tracks
Accounts New or dropped accounts, and changes to ARR, CSM, owner, Field CTO, region, adoption phase, or Request stage
Renewals New or closed renewals, and changes to risk, churn forecast, amount, or close date
Expansions New or closed expansions, and changes to amount or fiscal period
Churn Forecast Opportunities entering or leaving the churn forecast, field changes, and the net change in forecast churn dollars (an increase is shown in red)
CSM Assignments Accounts reassigned between CSMs
SaaS Pipeline Migrations added, removed, or updated
Escalations Jira escalations raised, closed, or changed in severity or status

Changed values are shown as old → new.

In the Churn Forecast section, “added” or “removed” usually means a deal’s churn quarter moved into or out of the forecast window. It doesn’t necessarily mean a deal was created or deleted.

CS Coverage

Each Customer Success Manager’s book of business. The Region pills filter it; every count below recalculates for the selected region.

The strip at the top totals the whole view: number of CSMs, accounts, ARR, accounts in S1 onboarding, Request retained, Active Request, and Ongoing Agentic Adoption.

Below it is one collapsible section per CSM, largest book (by ARR) first, with unassigned accounts last. Each section header shows:

ARR Total contract ARR across those accounts
PBAC Complexity Sum of the accounts’ PBAC complexity scores
S1 Onboarding Accounts in lifecycle stage S1 (highlighted when not zero)

Expand a section to see its accounts, each with the four-square adoption glyph, lifecycle stage, region, and ARR. Onboarding accounts are listed first and highlighted in amber with an Onboarding tag. Click an account name to open its card. Typing in the search box narrows the lists and opens the sections that contain a match.

2. Adoption

Two sub-tabs: Product Adoption Overview and How the Product Adoption Score Is Derived. The Region and CSM pills at the top apply to both the plot and the table.

Product Adoption Overview

Each account is scored on two separate measures and the two are crossed into one rating, because login counts alone don’t say much about adoption.

The scatter plot:

  • X axis: consumption ratio, registered users ÷ licensed users. Accounts above 1.25 are placed in a separate column at the right edge.
  • Y axis: product adoption score (0–10), PBAC complexity plus Request adoption points. See Product adoption score below.
  • Background shading shows the rating each region of the plot earns, and each point is colored by the region it falls in. See Product adoption rating below.

Reading a point:

  • Size reflects contract ARR.
  • Square markers are accounts in onboarding (lifecycle S1), where low usage is expected.
  • Dashed outline means the consumption ratio is unknown. The point is placed at zero only so it can be drawn.
  • Heavy outline means the ratio is above the axis maximum.
  • Dimmed points fall outside your current filters. They stay on the plot so you can see where your selection sits relative to the whole customer base.
  • Hover over a point to see its details in the side panel: the rating, ARR, the score broken into PBAC complexity and Request points, consumption, platforms, and PBAC features. Click to pin it there.

The table under the plot lists every account in view with all of its adoption and consumption data in one place:

Column Contents
Account, CSM, Region, ARR Account basics
Product Adoption The score, out of 10
Rating The product adoption rating, in full (e.g. Red: High Risk - Adoption)
Consumption Consumption ratio; hover for registered and licensed users
PBAC Complexity PBAC complexity score, out of 7
Request Request stage and the points it earns, e.g. Retained (+3)
Comply, Agentic The Salesforce Comply and Agentic Governance ratings
Platforms, IAM, Ext. Catalog Platforms in use, identity provider, and external catalog integrations
PBAC Features The PBAC complexity signals the account has earned
Lifecycle Stage Salesforce lifecycle stage

Every column sorts; it opens sorted by product adoption, highest first. Hovering a row rings that account’s point on the plot, and clicking a row pins it in the side panel. Onboarding (S1) accounts have an amber edge. The strip above the table shows how many accounts are in view, their average score, and how many fall in each rating.

How the Product Adoption Score Is Derived

The second sub-tab writes out the full method on the page itself. The same method is set out below.

Product adoption score

The score runs 0–10 and is the sum of two parts.

1. PBAC complexity (0–7). Five yes/no signals from the Looker consumption looks and the Domains dashboard. Only Production instances count.

Signal Points
Multi-platform (more than one platform in Production) 3
Both data and subscription policies active 1
Sensitive Data Discovery run in the last 90 days 1
Domains in use (governance is delegated) 1
At least one active guardrail policy 1

2. Request adoption (0–3). Points for the account’s Request stage. The stage comes from the Looker Request metrics dashboard where the account appears on it, and from the Salesforce Request Adoption picklist otherwise (green = retained, amber = adopted).

Request stage Points
Retained (in use for 3 of the last 4 weeks) 3
Adopted (use case in production, not yet retained) 1
Tried or none 0

A score of 4 or more counts as high adoption. Ways past it include multi-platform plus any one other signal or an adopted Request workflow, a retained Request workflow plus any one PBAC signal, or all four single-point PBAC signals. Multi-platform alone, or a retained Request workflow alone, scores 3 and does not clear it.

Product adoption rating

The score is crossed with the consumption ratio:

Ratio < 0.25 0.25 – 0.75 Ratio ≥ 0.75
Score ≥ 4 Red: High Risk - Consumption Amber: Moderate adoption Green: Strong adoption
Score < 4 Black: Critical Churn Risk Red: High Risk - Adoption Amber: Moderate adoption

Adoption alone caps at red, and consumption alone caps at amber. Neither reaches green by itself: green needs the deployment to be doing demanding work and people to be there doing it.

What the ratings call for:

  • Red: High Risk - Consumption (high score, few users): the platform is set up but people aren’t using it. This calls for enablement.
  • Red: High Risk - Adoption (low score, some users): people are using it, but only for simple work. This calls for deepening use cases.
  • Black: Critical Churn Risk: neither threshold is cleared and there is no adoption story to tell at renewal. These accounts need the most urgent attention.

How missing data is handled:

  • A missing PBAC signal counts as zero. For example, no Sensitive Data Discovery run means no point for that signal.
  • An unknown Request stage earns no points.
  • A missing consumption ratio stays unknown and isn’t treated as zero. On the score alone, such an account can reach red at most.
  • Zero licensed users means an ELA (unlimited seats). The ratio is treated as 100%.

PBAC rating vs. product adoption rating

The PBAC rating on each account card (the first square of the four-square glyph, and the mini plot on the card’s Overview tab) uses PBAC complexity alone (0–7) against consumption, with the same thresholds (4, 0.25, 0.75) and the same rating names. The product adoption rating on the Adoption tab adds Request on top, so an account can rate higher on the Adoption tab than on its card, never lower.

The Request, Comply, and Agentic ratings no longer have tabs of their own. They appear as columns in the Product Adoption Overview table and on each account card.

3. Performance

Self-Managed

All customers who aren’t on SaaS, with their Immuta version, support status (Supported, Unsupported, or Unknown), lifecycle stage, registered and licensed users, consumption ratio, data source count, and active policy count. You can filter by support status and lifecycle stage.

Rows that are highlighted are flagged Low Consumption, meaning at least one of these is true:

  • The account has no Production instance.
  • The consumption ratio is below 25%.
  • The Production instance has zero active policies.

SaaS Migration

A board of self-managed customers moving to SaaS, with one column per estimated migration quarter. The current quarter is marked · now, and migrations without a scheduled quarter appear in an Unscheduled column.

  • Each card shows the account, region, CSM, pipeline stage (Pipeline → Having conversations → Verbal commitment → Migration in progress), and the date it’s expected to move to the next stage. That date turns red with (past) if it has passed. Click a card to read its notes.
  • Assumed churn tags mark customers who are expected to leave rather than migrate.
  • TGT SaaS % at the top of each column is the projected share of customers on SaaS by the end of that quarter: (SaaS customers today + migrations due by that quarter) ÷ (all customers − expected churn by that quarter). It follows the Region and CSM filters but ignores the search box.
  • The summary figures at the top show SaaS today (the current share) and how many migrations are at each stage.

Escalations

Open CustX Jira issues, sorted by severity and then by age (oldest first). You can filter by severity, status, region, and CSM. Each row shows the Jira key (linked), summary, account, status, assignee, and when it was opened. Issues open for 90 days or more are highlighted as stale. “No account matched” means the Jira issue couldn’t be tied to a Salesforce account.
